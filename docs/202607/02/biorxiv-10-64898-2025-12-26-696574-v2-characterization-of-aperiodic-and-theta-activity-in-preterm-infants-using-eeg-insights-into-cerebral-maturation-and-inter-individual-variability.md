## 摘要
早产干扰大脑发育的关键过程，阻碍脑网络和功能活动的成熟，其中包括被认为在早期网络形成中起关键作用的θ振荡。传统的EEG频谱分析显示婴儿早期θ功率显著发育，但这些方法混合了振荡活动与非振荡活动，限制了对神经变化背后机制的理解。通过频谱参数化，我们旨在评估早产儿与足月儿相比，非周期性活动和周期性θ功率的发育变化，并进一步探究临床因素和脑微结构能否解释早产儿内部的个体间变异性。

在41名极早产儿（出生时胎龄<32周；平均值±标准差：26.9±1.7周）和13名足月对照儿（出生时胎龄≥37周；40.1±1周）中，于足月等效年龄（TEA）和校正年龄2个月（2mCA）时，在活跃/快速眼动睡眠期间采集了高密度EEG。使用频谱参数化提取全脑和空间电极簇（前部、中央、后部）的非周期性成分（偏移、指数）和周期性θ功率。从TEA到2mCA，偏移、指数和θ功率均增加，早产儿与足月儿之间无差异。在TEA时，前部区域的非周期性活动指标强于后部区域，但到2mCA时，这种区域性分布发生转变，后部区域的非周期性偏移和指数从TEA到2mCA显著增加。在早产儿内部，TEA时非周期性和周期性活动的个体间变异性部分可由临床风险因素解释：男性、出生时胎龄较低、出生体重低以及有创通气与非周期性偏移、指数和θ功率的变化相关。此外，TEA时较高的θ功率与同一时期弥散MRI评估的较低皮层各向异性分数相关，这与大脑更成熟的发育一致。总之，这些发现表明，结合空间分析的EEG频谱参数化提供了一个灵敏的框架，可用于表征婴儿出生后大脑活动的成熟，以及与早产和围产期逆境相关的早期脆弱性。

## Abstract
Disrupting critical processes of brain development, preterm birth interferes with the maturation of brain networks and functional activity, including theta oscillations that are thought to play a key role in early network formation. Traditional EEG spectral analyses have indicated marked development of theta power in early infancy, but these approaches mix oscillatory and non-oscillatory activity, limiting insights into the mechanisms underlying the neural changes. Using spectral parameterization, we aimed to evaluate developmental changes in aperiodic activity and periodic theta power in infants born very preterm compared to full-terms, and to further explore whether clinical factors and brain microstructure could explain the inter-individual variability within preterms.

High-density EEG was acquired during active/REM sleep at term-equivalent age (TEA) and 2 months corrected age (2mCA) in 41 very preterm infants (born <32 weeks gestational age [GA]; mean {+/-} standard deviation: 26.9{+/-}1.7 weeks) and 13 full-term controls (born [&ge;]37 weeks GA; 40.1{+/-}1 weeks). Spectral parameterization was used to extract aperiodic components (offset, exponent) and periodic theta power globally and across spatial clusters of electrodes (anterior, central, posterior). From TEA to 2mCA, offset, exponent, and theta power increased with no differences between preterm and full-term infants. At TEA, metrics of aperiodic activity were stronger in anterior compared with posterior areas, but this regional landscape shifted by 2mCA with pronounced increases in aperiodic offset and exponent in posterior areas from TEA to 2mCA. Within preterms, inter-individual variability in aperiodic and periodic activity at TEA was partly explained by clinical risk factors: male sex, lower gestational-age at birth, small weight at birth, and invasive ventilation were linked to alterations of aperiodic offset, exponent and theta power. Additionally, higher theta power at TEA correlated with lower cortical fractional anisotropy assessed with diffusion MRI at the same age, consistently with more advanced maturation of the brain. Collectively, these findings indicate that EEG spectral parameterization combined with spatial analysis provides a sensitive framework for characterizing the postnatal maturation of brain activity in infants, as well as early vulnerabilities associated with prematurity and perinatal adversity.
